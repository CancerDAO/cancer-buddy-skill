# Advance Care Planning in China

Advance care planning (ACP) is the process by which a person, while they have decisional capacity, reflects on and communicates their values and preferences for future medical care — especially for scenarios in which they may no longer be able to speak for themselves. In China, the legal and institutional infrastructure for ACP is developing unevenly across provinces.

This reference outlines the process, the distinctions between commonly-used terms, the current province-level legal status (as of the author's best understanding in early 2026 — verify with a local attorney for legally binding steps), and practical steps.

**Important:** legal frameworks evolve. The authoritative source for a specific document's legal effect is a Chinese attorney familiar with the relevant province's most current rules. This reference is orientation, not legal advice.

## The ACP process

ACP is a process, not a single document. It has three broad stages:

1. **Discussion** — values clarification between patient, family, and ideally treating clinicians. "What matters to you at end of life? What would a good death look like for you? If you could not communicate, whose judgment would you most trust to speak for you?"
2. **Documentation** — writing down the preferences, either informally (letter to family, kept with medical records) or formally (signed witnessed document, notarized document).
3. **Legal or operational status** — depending on province and document type, the written preferences may have varying degrees of binding legal force in later medical decision-making.

ACP conversations are most useful when they are ongoing, not one-time. Values shift with disease trajectory. Early ACP conversations set a foundation; late-stage conversations refine specifics (intubation preferences, CPR preferences, preferred place of death).

## Key distinctions

### 生前预嘱 (Living will)

A written document in which a person, while competent, states their preferences for medical treatment if they become terminally ill or incapable of speaking for themselves. Typically covers:

- Preferences about CPR / intubation / ICU admission at end of life
- Preferences about artificial nutrition and hydration in terminal illness
- Preferences about pain control even if sedating
- Wishes about place of death (home / hospice / hospital)
- Wishes about organ / body donation
- Identification of a surrogate decision-maker

In China, the 生前预嘱 concept has been promoted by civil-society groups (notably 选择与尊严 / Beijing Living Will Promotion Association since 2013), and progressively codified at province level.

### 预立医疗指令 (Advance medical directive)

Often used interchangeably with 生前预嘱, but can be narrower — specifically a medical-instructions document (e.g., a POLST-style form) rather than a broader values statement.

### DNR (Do Not Resuscitate) / 不实施心肺复苏

A specific clinical order, typically signed by a patient (or surrogate) and entered into the medical record. Directs that CPR not be attempted in cardiac arrest. In Chinese hospitals this is often a specific in-chart decision, not a standalone legal document.

### 医疗委托 / 授权 (Medical proxy / surrogate)

Designation of a specific person to make medical decisions on behalf of the patient if they lose capacity. Legally distinct from a 生前预嘱 content statement. May require notarization for unambiguous legal status.

### 遗嘱 (Will)

**Not the same as 生前预嘱.** A will is for property / assets after death. A 生前预嘱 is for medical care while alive but incapacitated. These are separate documents with separate legal frameworks. See `death-logistics.md` for the will side.

## Province-level legal status (approximate, verify locally)

### Beijing

- 选择与尊严协会 (Beijing Living Will Promotion Association) has operated since 2013, providing templates and advocacy. The online template ("我的五个愿望") is widely used.
- At the municipal level, 生前预嘱 has operational recognition in many hospitals but varied legal binding force before the Shenzhen law (see below).

### Shenzhen (深圳)

- **First province-level law (2022):** the 深圳经济特区医疗条例 (2022 amendment) included provisions giving legal effect to 生前预嘱 in Shenzhen. Widely viewed as the first formal legal basis for living wills in mainland China.

### Shanghai (上海)

- 2024 amendment to local health regulations extended 生前预嘱 recognition. Implementation details continue to evolve.

### Other provinces

- Most other provinces as of early 2026 do not have a dedicated province-level 生前预嘱 statute. The document still has evidentiary and ethical weight (a properly witnessed document is strong evidence of the patient's prior expressed wishes), but enforceability is discretionary.
- Practical implication: a well-documented, witnessed, family-known 生前预嘱 is operationally useful almost everywhere, even without a specific statute.

**Caveat:** this is a rapidly changing landscape. Verify the latest status for the patient's province before treating a document as legally binding.

## Practical steps

### For the patient (while capacity intact)

1. **Reflect on values.** What does "living well" mean to you? What interventions would you accept for what benefit? Where do you want to be when you die?
2. **Talk to family.** The people who will be at your bedside need to know your wishes, not just read them after.
3. **Document.** Use a structured template. The "我的五个愿望" (五个愿望) template from 选择与尊严 covers the common ground: (1) wanted / unwanted medical care, (2) life-support preferences, (3) comfort desires, (4) how I want to be treated by loved ones, (5) what I want my loved ones to know.
4. **Sign with witnesses.** Two adult witnesses who are NOT the designated surrogate, NOT beneficiaries under the patient's will.
5. **Consider notarization (公证)** for stronger legal weight — required in some provinces, strongly recommended elsewhere for unambiguous standing.
6. **Designate a surrogate** — document who can speak for you if you can't.
7. **Give copies** to surrogate, primary family, treating physician, and keep in medical record.
8. **Revisit** at major disease milestones (new diagnosis, progression, treatment change). Preferences can and do evolve.

### For the family

- Initiate the conversation, don't wait for the patient to bring it up. Many patients feel relief that "finally someone is willing to talk about this."
- Don't treat disagreement with the patient's wishes as refusal to follow them. The patient's stated wishes (if documented while competent) bind the family morally, even where not yet legally binding.
- Bring the document to the hospital admission. Ask it be scanned into the chart.

### For the treating team

- Ask: "Has the patient done any advance care planning? Is there a 生前预嘱 we should have in the chart?"
- When there's a document, honor it within applicable law and ethics. When there isn't, facilitate one if the patient is still competent and the trajectory suggests upcoming incapacity.

## Template language (excerpt, illustrative only)

> 本人 [姓名], 身份证号 [XXX], 在意识清醒、具备完全民事行为能力的情况下，基于对自己医疗事务的慎重考虑，作出如下预嘱：
>
> 若本人处于临终状态（经两名及以上医师判断为不可逆、治疗效果有限、预期生存期较短），且已失去表达意愿的能力时:
>
> 1. 关于心肺复苏（CPR）：[希望实施 / 不希望实施]
> 2. 关于气管插管和机械通气：[希望实施 / 不希望实施 / 仅在短期可逆情况下接受]
> 3. 关于人工营养与水分: [希望接受 / 不希望接受 / 仅在明显改善舒适度时接受]
> 4. 关于疼痛控制: 即使可能有镇静副作用, 我希望医护充分控制我的疼痛。
> 5. 关于就医地点偏好: [家中 / 安宁疗护机构 / 医院]
> 6. 本人指定 [姓名] 为医疗决策代理人, 在本人失去表达能力时代为沟通上述意愿。
>
> 本预嘱在见证人 [姓名 1, 2] 在场下亲笔签署, 日期 [YYYY-MM-DD].
>
> [签名] [见证人签名 × 2]

*The above is illustrative. Use a validated template (e.g., 选择与尊严的"我的五个愿望") and consult locally for witness / notarization requirements.*
