# Pain Management and Opiophobia Correction

Cancer pain in China is systematically under-treated, and the primary driver is not clinical — it is fear. Fear of addiction, fear of respiratory depression, fear of "needing more and more," fear of being labeled "weak" for needing opioids. Some of this fear comes from families, some from patients, some (surprisingly) from clinicians in non-palliative specialties who haven't kept up with the evidence. This reference exists to correct the fear with data.

## WHO 三阶梯 — still the standard

The World Health Organization's cancer pain ladder has been the global standard for cancer pain management since 1986, with refinements over the years. It remains central.

### Step 1 — Non-opioid ± adjuvant

- **Non-opioids:** paracetamol (对乙酰氨基酚), NSAIDs (布洛芬 / 萘普生 / 塞来昔布)
- **NSAIDs cautions:** renal impairment (eGFR < 30), GI ulcer / bleed risk, concomitant anticoagulation, platelet dysfunction, heart failure
- **Adjuvants:** gabapentin / pregabalin for neuropathic component, antidepressants (TCAs, SNRIs) for some neuropathic pain, steroids (dexamethasone) for bone pain / nerve compression / capsular distension, bisphosphonates or denosumab for bone metastases

### Step 2 — Weak opioid ± non-opioid ± adjuvant

- **Tramadol (曲马多):** weak opioid + SNRI effect. Note CYP2D6 metabolism variability, seizure risk at high dose, serotonergic interactions.
- **Codeine:** less used in modern oncology; CYP2D6 variability affects efficacy.
- **Modern practice** often bypasses Step 2 and moves from Step 1 to low-dose Step 3 for moderate-severe cancer pain.

### Step 3 — Strong opioid ± non-opioid ± adjuvant

- **Morphine (吗啡):** the reference strong opioid. Available 口服 immediate-release, 口服 sustained-release, 皮下 (SC), 静脉 (IV). Broadly accessible.
- **Oxycodone (羟考酮, 奥施康定 is common brand):** good oral bioavailability, often used in China.
- **Hydromorphone:** more potent per mg, available IV / SC; useful when high doses needed in small volumes.
- **Fentanyl (芬太尼):**
  - **Transdermal patch (芬太尼贴剂)** is the most common use in China — useful for STABLE chronic cancer pain when oral route no longer feasible (dysphagia, N/V, obstruction). Avoid in opioid-naive patients (risk of over-dose), not for acute or rapidly changing pain (slow onset / offset).
  - IV / SC fentanyl — specialist settings.
  - Buccal / sublingual rapid-onset fentanyl preparations exist internationally; availability in mainland China is limited.
- **Methadone (美沙酮):** effective in neuropathic pain, affordable, but complex half-life with risk of accumulation and QT prolongation. Specialist use only — not first-line unless clinician is experienced.

### Adjuvants at any step

- Neuropathic: gabapentin, pregabalin, duloxetine, nortriptyline
- Bone: bisphosphonates, denosumab, steroids
- Nerve compression / cord / capsular: steroids (dexamethasone)
- Anxiety amplifying pain: address anxiety separately

## Opioid facts — what the evidence actually says

### New opioid dependence in cancer pain

- Multiple large studies and systematic reviews: new opioid use disorder in patients treated with opioids for cancer pain is **rare — typically < 1%**. This is in contrast to the chronic non-cancer pain literature, where rates are higher and form the basis of the "opioid crisis" narrative in Western media.
- The cancer context matters: the condition itself shortens exposure, the pain has an organic driver, and the social / psychiatric risk factors that predispose to opioid use disorder are distributed independently of cancer diagnosis.
- Important: a patient with a pre-existing substance use disorder is a higher risk — but still deserves cancer pain control, just with additional care planning.

### Tolerance vs dependence vs addiction (important distinctions)

- **Tolerance** — requiring higher doses over time to achieve the same effect. Normal, expected, NOT addiction.
- **Physical dependence** — withdrawal symptoms if abruptly stopped. Normal physiologic adaptation, NOT addiction.
- **Addiction (use disorder)** — compulsive use despite harm, loss of control, continued use despite negative consequences. This is the rare outcome.

Tolerance and physical dependence are normal and expected in chronic opioid therapy. They do not mean the patient is "becoming addicted."

### Respiratory depression

- Clinically significant respiratory depression in carefully titrated opioid therapy for cancer pain is rare. It is more likely with rapid dose escalation, opioid-naive patients given high doses, or combination with other CNS depressants (benzos, alcohol).
- Morphine for breath hunger (dyspnea) uses LOW doses — does not cause respiratory depression at these doses.

### "老人会更危险吗"

- Older patients need more careful dose titration (renal clearance, comorbidities, cognitive reserve) — but "older" is not a contraindication. Under-treating pain in elderly cancer patients is common and unethical.

## Responding to family questions

### "老人家会上瘾吗?"

> "这是特别多家属的担心。但有大量研究数据：癌痛患者规律使用阿片类药物出现成瘾的新发率在 1% 以下。癌痛下的用药，和毒品成瘾是两回事。身体可能会有耐受性（同样剂量效果减弱）和生理依赖（突然停药会不舒服），这是正常的药理现象，不是成瘾。我们按医生的计划加量、换药、减量，都是安全可控的。"

### "吗啡是不是很危险, 不到最后不能用?"

> "吗啡不是"最后一步"。在 WHO 方案里, 对中重度癌痛就是规范用药. 用早一点反而让病人少受罪, 也不会让之后"无药可用" — 医生可以换药、调剂量、加辅助药物，还有很多空间。'熬到最后才用'在过去是常见做法, 但现在的证据不支持."

### "会不会用了就下不来了?"

> "癌症治疗过程中，如果疼痛原因被控制了（比如肿瘤缩小、放疗生效、手术解除压迫）, 阿片类药物可以逐步减量、甚至停用. 减量有规范方法, 不是'突然停'. 让医生带着一起减是安全的."

### "打针/贴片会让人成为瘾君子吗?"

> "不会. 贴片（比如芬太尼贴）、皮下或静脉给药是针对特定情况 — 比如口服不能用、需要快速起效、或者剂量稳定. 给药路线不决定成瘾风险, 使用场景和剂量控制才决定."

## Breakthrough pain (爆发痛)

- **Definition:** transient exacerbation of pain over a background of stable controlled pain on regular opioid regimen.
- **Rescue dose:** typically 10-15% of the 24-hour total opioid dose, given as immediate-release, repeatable as needed (often q1-2h prn).
- **Frequency of rescues:** if patient needs > 3-4 rescues per day regularly, the baseline regimen likely needs upward titration.
- **Route of rescue:** immediate-release oral, SC, or in some settings rapid-onset transmucosal.
- **Document:** each breakthrough dose in the symptom log (see `symptom-management-end-of-life.md` and the YAML schema in `patient-profile-schema.md`).

## Transdermal fentanyl (芬太尼贴剂)

- **When to use:** stable chronic cancer pain in patients who cannot take oral (dysphagia, intractable N/V, obstruction, impaired consciousness but sufficient subcutaneous perfusion).
- **When NOT to use:**
  - Opioid-naive patients (risk of over-dose — reservoir-based release)
  - Acute / rapidly escalating pain (slow onset, ~12h to reach effect)
  - Fever or heat exposure (increases release — risk of over-dose)
  - Cachexia severe enough to compromise SC depot
- **Rotation from oral morphine:** use established equianalgesic conversion; the standard references used in China are tables published by 国家卫健委 / 肿瘤科学会 guidance. Always titrate after conversion, do not assume exact dose equivalence.
- **Changing:** patches typically every 72h (some formulations 48h); rotate application site.
- **Breakthrough:** patients on patches still need immediate-release rescue meds for breakthrough pain — patches do not cover acute exacerbations.

## End-of-life IV / SC infusion

- When oral route fails and transdermal isn't enough / appropriate, continuous IV or SC infusion (often with a PCA — 患者自控镇痛) is an option.
- Can be delivered in hospital, hospice, or (in some areas) home hospice settings.
- Provides stable background analgesia with patient- or family-activated breakthrough boluses.

## Red flags in pain management

- **Sudden severe new pain** — especially back / spinal — cord compression emergency, imaging urgently.
- **New neurological deficit** — cord, brain metastasis, hemorrhage.
- **Opioid toxicity signs** — myoclonus (twitching), confusion / delirium, pinpoint pupils, respiratory rate < 8-10. Consider dose reduction, hydration, rotation.
- **Escalation outpacing prescription** — evaluate for disease progression, incident pain, psychological amplification, diversion (rare in this population but not zero).

## Key citation base (representative)

- WHO guidelines for the pharmacological treatment of pain in cancer (WHO 2018 update of the ladder).
- 中国抗癌协会 / 中华医学会: 癌症疼痛诊疗规范 (various editions, most recent at time of consulting).
- Studies on new opioid use disorder rates in cancer populations: multiple large cohort analyses in US / European literature consistently < 1% for new dependence arising from cancer-pain opioid treatment.
- Fishbain et al., and others, on distinction between tolerance, dependence, addiction.
