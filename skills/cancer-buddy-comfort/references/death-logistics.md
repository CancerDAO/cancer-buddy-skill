# Death Logistics — Practical Handling

At end of life, families are grieving and have to handle a surprising amount of logistics. Much of this is practical, bureaucratic, and solvable — but hard to think about from scratch in crisis. This reference is orientation, not legal or funeral-industry advice; specific processes vary by city and institution.

## Place of death — home vs hospital vs hospice

### Home

**Potential upsides**
- Familiar environment, pets, favorite things nearby
- Family can be present on their own schedule
- Patient often feels more at peace
- Less institutional; more personal

**Potential downsides**
- Requires family caregiver capacity (physical + emotional)
- Symptom crises (severe pain, dyspnea, delirium) harder to manage without on-call clinical team
- Body handling after death requires calling 120 (ambulance) + 殡仪馆 — some families find this harder at home than in hospital
- Not every community supports home death well (varies by city, district, and whether home hospice service is available)

**Practical prep**
- Ensure adequate supply of symptom meds (especially breakthrough pain, anti-secretion)
- Have hospice team / 社区医生 contact available 24/7 if possible
- Agree in advance who will be present, who handles post-death logistics
- Keep copies of 生前预嘱 / DNR documents at home
- Know in advance which 殡仪馆 to call and their number

### Hospital

**Potential upsides**
- Clinical team on hand for symptom crises
- Death certification and body-handling logistics handled institutionally
- Some patients feel safer there
- Better option if caregiver capacity at home is limited

**Potential downsides**
- Less personal
- Visiting restrictions in some hospitals
- Risk of unwanted interventions (if 生前预嘱 / DNR not clearly documented, staff may default to resuscitation)
- Higher cost in some cases (varies)

**Practical prep**
- Ensure DNR / 生前预嘱 is in the chart, not just in a file somewhere
- Discuss with attending what "comfort care" admission means at this hospital
- Request a private room if available
- Ask about palliative team involvement

### Inpatient hospice (安宁疗护)

**Potential upsides**
- Purpose-built for end-of-life care
- Team trained in symptom management and family support
- Often more welcoming environment than acute hospital
- Culture of not over-intervening

**Potential downsides**
- Availability uneven across China (see `hospice-in-china.md`)
- Cost varies
- Some families fear the "move to hospice" as "giving up"

### Home hospice service

- Combines home setting with clinical support
- Availability limited to pilot-city areas mostly
- When available, often the best of both worlds

## Right after death — the first hours

### Step 1: Death certification (医院死亡证明 / 居民死亡医学证明书)

- **In hospital / hospice:** attending physician issues 医院死亡证明 / 居民死亡医学证明书 (官方文件). This is the foundational document for everything that follows.
- **At home:** if under home hospice with a responsible physician, that physician can certify. Otherwise, family typically calls 120 — the responding physician will certify. If death occurs without clinical oversight, some areas require police notification / forensic examination to rule out non-natural cause. Know in advance what the local process is.

### Step 2: Call the funeral home (殡仪馆 / 殡葬服务中心)

- Urban China's standard process: call the local 殡仪馆 (most cities have a designated municipal facility; 一线城市 may have multiple).
- They dispatch a vehicle to collect the body.
- Discuss arrangements (immediate 火化 vs short hold for family visits, 告别仪式 planning).
- Cost varies: basic 火化 + 告别仪式 is often in the range of a few thousand to tens of thousands 元 depending on city and options.

### Step 3: Preserve the body briefly if needed

- Cooling / refrigeration at 殡仪馆 (or some hospitals have morgue hold) allows for family to gather.
- Duration typically 1-3 days in most settings; longer is possible but costs more.

### Step 4: 告别仪式 / memorial

- Format varies: simple 告别 at 殡仪馆 is the most common urban Chinese form.
- Religious / cultural customs as the family wishes — Buddhist chanting, Christian service, Muslim burial process (different — see below), secular memorial, any combination.
- Note: Muslim families have distinct requirements (typically prompt burial, ritual washing / 净礼); coordinate with local mosque and 殡仪馆 Muslim services where applicable.

## 火化 vs 土葬

- Urban mainland China: **火化 is effectively universal** (cremation policy, enforced in most cities). Exceptions exist in some rural areas and for specific ethnic / religious minorities (回民, some minzu areas).
- 骨灰 is interred in 公墓 (cemetery), scattered (where permitted), or held in 骨灰堂.
- 墓地 cost in major cities has risen sharply; plan if possible.

## 户籍注销 and other bureaucratic follow-ups

After 死亡证明 issued, family member handles:

- **户籍注销** — bring 死亡证明 to 户籍所在地派出所. Within a reasonable time after death (practices vary; typically weeks to a couple months — check local rules).
- **医保卡 / 社保卡 注销** — coordinate with 社保 and 医保 offices; some processes automatic after 户籍注销, some manual.
- **退休金 / 养老金** processing — 社保局.
- **Bank accounts** — banks generally freeze upon notification of death; 继承公证 (notarized inheritance) may be needed to release funds.
- **房产 / 车辆 过户** — needs 继承公证 or 遗嘱 + 过户 process via relevant bureau.
- **身份证 / passport** — typically collected or voided at 户籍注销 stage.

**Timeline**: allow several weeks to a few months for the full financial / property cleanup depending on complexity. Grief and bureaucracy do not play well together; consider having one sibling / relative take lead while others have space to grieve.

## 遗嘱 (will) vs 生前预嘱 (living will)

Critical distinction:

| | 生前预嘱 | 遗嘱 |
|---|---|---|
| **When operative** | While alive but incapacitated | After death |
| **Subject matter** | Medical care preferences | Property / asset distribution |
| **Legal framework** | Health regulations (province-level, developing) | 民法典 继承编 (national) |
| **Format** | Written, witnessed; notarization often recommended | Multiple valid forms per 民法典: 自书 / 代书 / 打印 / 录音录像 / 公证 / 口头 (emergency) |

**Both are useful. Neither replaces the other.** Encourage the patient to consider both while competent. See `advance-care-planning-china.md` for 生前预嘱 process.

For 遗嘱: a notarized (公证) will provides strongest legal standing; if not notarized, 自书 with witnesses is still valid under 民法典 with proper formalities. Consult a Chinese attorney for specifics if meaningful assets are involved.

## Digital legacy

Increasingly important — people's digital lives hold value (financial, sentimental, or both).

- **Bank / 支付宝 / 微信 accounts** — typically frozen on death notification. 继承公证 may be needed. Alipay and WeChat Pay each have their own inheritance processes; check their official help for latest procedures.
- **Apple ID / 华为 ID / 小米 ID** — Apple has a "Legacy Contact" feature (2021+); other platforms evolving. Without documented access, family may lose photos, messages, etc.
- **Passwords / 2FA** — consider a sealed written record of essential passwords (bank, primary email) kept with the 遗嘱 or with a trusted family member. This is not legally required but practically invaluable.
- **Social media / chat history** — some platforms offer memorialization or deletion on request with death proof.
- **Cryptocurrency / online assets** — if the decedent held any, private key / seed phrase access is make-or-break. Document (safely) while alive.

**Practical suggestion:** while the patient is able, have a conversation: "If something happened, would you want [partner / child] to be able to access [photos / WeChat / bank / etc.]? How should we set it up?" This is not morbid; it is kind.

## Financial cleanup timeline (approximate)

| Timing | Task |
|---|---|
| Day 0 | 死亡证明 issued; 殡仪馆 contacted |
| Day 1-3 | 告别仪式 / 火化 arranged and completed |
| Week 1-2 | 户籍注销; notify workplace / 社保 / 医保 / 退休金 |
| Week 2-4 | Identify assets / debts; begin inheritance process; attorney consult if meaningful estate |
| Month 1-3 | 继承公证 or 诉讼继承 as needed; bank / property transfers |
| Month 3+ | Finalize transfers; close remaining accounts |

Timelines vary. Grief takes longer than paperwork.

## Personal / emotional logistics

Often overlooked, always important:

- **Belongings** — clothing, papers, possessions. Family members often disagree on pacing (keep everything / clear quickly). Negotiate. A middle path is usual: let the closest mourner pace it; hold things for a time; let the mourner revisit.
- **Photos / heirlooms** — decide distribution before disputes arise; if possible, patient input while competent is gold.
- **Bereavement support** — if the hospice team offered follow-up, use it. Otherwise: 心理咨询, 支持小组, religious community, or even online groups for specific cancer types.
