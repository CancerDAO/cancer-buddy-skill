# Hospice (安宁疗护) in China — Policy, Resources, and Entry

Hospice care in mainland China has moved from boutique / pilot to policy-backed expansion over the past decade. The pace is uneven across regions, and access is still far from uniform, but the policy framework is real and worth understanding.

## Policy timeline (highlights)

- **2017** — National Health Commission (卫健委) launched the first wave of 安宁疗护 pilots in 5 cities (Beijing Haidian, Jilin Changchun, Shanghai Pudong, Henan Luohe, Sichuan Deyang).
- **2019** — 国家卫生健康委员会办公厅 published 国卫办发 [2019] 35 号 "安宁疗护中心基本标准（试行）" and "安宁疗护实践指南（试行）" — defined what a hospice unit must have (staffing, facilities, multi-disciplinary team) and the scope of hospice practice (symptom management, psychosocial care, spiritual care, bereavement support).
- **2019-2021** — pilot expanded to second wave of 71 cities.
- **2022** — 深圳经济特区医疗条例 amendment gave 生前预嘱 legal effect — a complementary piece, strengthening the ACP → hospice pipeline.
- **2023-2026** — ongoing integration with 医共体 (medical consortia) and community health centers; more provinces adding dedicated hospice beds; uneven but expanding.

**What this means:** a patient in a tier-1 city or a pilot area can usually find a hospice service. Outside those areas, access may require travel or creative routing (see below).

## What hospice does (and doesn't)

### Hospice provides:

- **Symptom management** — pain, dyspnea, nausea, delirium, secretions, constipation, fatigue, anxiety, insomnia (see `symptom-management-end-of-life.md`)
- **Multi-disciplinary team** — physician, nurse, sometimes social worker, chaplain / spiritual care, psychologist, volunteers
- **Family support** — communication facilitation, respite, preparation for death, bereavement contact (variable by program)
- **Place-of-care coordination** — inpatient hospice, home hospice, hospital hospice unit
- **Advance care planning conversations** — if not already done
- **Honest prognostic information** (within culturally-appropriate framing, see `family-talk-scripts.md` and `cancer-buddy-disclosure`)

### Hospice generally does NOT provide (in the mainland China context):

- Active anti-cancer treatment (chemotherapy for life-prolongation, aggressive radiation for life-prolongation rather than symptom palliation, targeted therapy as life-extending). Palliative radiation for specific symptom control (e.g., painful bone metastasis) may be provided.
- ICU-level interventions (intubation for mechanical ventilation, CPR, dialysis started anew).
- Euthanasia / assisted dying — NOT legal in mainland China.
- Curative-intent interventions.

Entry to hospice typically means the patient (or surrogate) has oriented toward comfort as the primary goal of care. This is a conversation, not a switch.

## Where to find hospice in China

### Tier-1 / pilot cities

- **Tier-1 大三甲医院缓和医疗科 / 安宁疗护科** — many major hospitals now have a palliative / hospice service. Examples: 北京协和, 北京朝阳, 上海复旦肿瘤, 四川大学华西, 中山大学肿瘤防治中心. Call the hospital's 门诊办 or 安宁疗护科.
- **Community health centers with hospice beds** — part of the pilot program. Ask the street / district 社区卫生服务中心 directly about 安宁疗护床位.
- **Private / non-profit hospices** — e.g., Beijing 松堂关怀医院 (one of the earliest), some municipal / provincial dedicated hospice hospitals. Non-profit hospices vary widely in quality; reference checks via patient groups help.
- **Home hospice services** — coordinated by hospital teams or community centers in pilot areas. Less common outside pilots.

### Non-pilot areas

- Often, the closest approximation is the 缓和医疗 / 疼痛科 service at the treating hospital, even without a dedicated hospice unit. This can provide symptom-management support.
- Some families travel to pilot-city hospices for the end-of-life phase. This has logistical and financial implications — weigh against staying in familiar environment.
- Home-based care supported by a willing community physician + family — possible but requires a knowledgeable local doctor for opioid prescribing and symptom orders.

## Cost (ballpark, varies widely)

- **Public inpatient hospice, pilot-city** — roughly 2,000-5,000 元/月 patient copay after 医保 coverage for eligible stays (highly variable by local medical insurance policy and bed type).
- **Private / non-profit inpatient hospice** — roughly 5,000-15,000 元/月, often out-of-pocket. High-end urban privates can be higher.
- **Home hospice service** — variable; nursing visit fees + medication + equipment. Typically less expensive than inpatient but requires family caregiver capacity.
- **大三甲 inpatient palliative bed (symptom stay)** — covered under standard inpatient 医保, often cheapest route when applicable.

**Medical insurance (医保) coverage** for hospice is a patchwork. Pilot cities have experimented with hospice-specific 医保 categories; outside pilots, patients often use general inpatient coverage or pay out-of-pocket. Verify with the specific institution.

## Entry criteria (typical)

- **Prognosis** — estimated life expectancy ≤ 6 months (clinical judgment, not a strict test). Tools: Palliative Performance Scale (PPS), ECOG, functional decline rate.
- **Goals of care** — patient (or surrogate) oriented toward comfort, not active anti-cancer treatment (exceptions for palliative-intent treatments).
- **Capacity for outpatient / home care assessed** — for home hospice, family caregiver capacity + willingness.
- **Documentation** — medical records confirming advanced disease; sometimes a DNR / 生前预嘱 in chart; referral letter from treating oncologist.

## Common misconceptions

**"进了 hospice 就没有医疗了"**

Wrong. Hospice IS medical care — just with a different focus. Pain meds, symptom-control meds, nursing visits, physician visits. What changes is the orientation.

**"送 hospice 就是不孝顺"**

A difficult cultural frame. Reframe: "选择 hospice 是让 Ta 舒服、有尊严地走完最后一段，这是爱的一种形式。" Not every family will accept this framing; meet them where they are.

**"Home hospice 不安全"**

Depends on symptom burden and caregiver capacity. For stable symptom profiles with capable family, home hospice is often preferred by patients. For complex symptoms (refractory pain, terminal delirium, high-flow secretions), inpatient hospice may be safer.

**"进 hospice 就出不来了"**

Not necessarily. Some patients stabilize and discharge (transition to home). Some change their minds and return to active treatment (complex logistics). Hospice entry is serious but not irreversible.

## Practical referral language

When asking the treating team:

> "我们想了解 Ta 的病情是否适合考虑 安宁疗护 / 缓和医疗 服务。能否安排缓和医疗科会诊? 或者您能否推荐当地合适的 hospice 资源？"

When calling a hospice service directly:

> "您好, 我想咨询 安宁疗护 的收治情况。我的家人 [诊断], 目前 [大致情况]。请问怎么评估是否符合入住标准？需要准备什么材料？"
