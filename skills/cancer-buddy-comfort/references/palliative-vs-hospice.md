# Palliative Care vs Hospice — What's the Difference?

The terms "缓和医疗" (palliative) and "安宁疗护 / 临终关怀" (hospice) are often conflated in China, and that confusion has real cost. Families avoid palliative referrals because they sound like "giving up" — when in fact palliative care alongside anti-cancer treatment is current standard of care in stage IV disease, and has an independent survival benefit.

This reference exists to clarify the distinction and common misconceptions.

## The distinction (table)

| Dimension | Palliative care (缓和医疗) | Hospice (安宁疗护 / 临终关怀) |
|---|---|---|
| **When** | Any stage, any time — ideally from diagnosis in advanced disease | Generally when life expectancy is estimated at ≤ 6 months |
| **Concurrent with anti-cancer treatment?** | Yes, usually is | No — hospice entry typically means no further active anti-cancer therapy |
| **Goal** | Symptom control + QoL, alongside curative / life-prolonging care | Comfort and dignity in the final months |
| **Setting** | Outpatient clinic, inpatient consult, home visits | Inpatient hospice, home hospice, day hospice |
| **Who delivers** | Palliative medicine team (缓和医疗科 / 疼痛科 / 肿瘤科) | Hospice team (often multi-disciplinary including social work, spiritual care) |
| **Cost in China** | Usually covered by 医保 for outpatient visits + inpatient symptom stays | Mixed — some medical insurance coverage for inpatient hospice in pilot cities; much is out-of-pocket still |
| **Evidence for early initiation** | Temel et al., NEJM 2010: early palliative in metastatic NSCLC → longer survival AND better QoL | Hospice enrollment in last weeks associated with fewer hospitalizations, family better-prepared |

## When each is right

### Palliative care is appropriate when:

- **Any advanced cancer** — stage IV solid tumor, relapsed hematologic, significant symptom burden
- **ECOG ≥ 2** — functional decline present, interventions may be more about quality than length
- **Symptom burden despite active treatment** — uncontrolled pain, dyspnea, nausea, fatigue
- **Treatment decision complexity** — helping patients and families weigh options, values clarification
- **Caregiver distress** — the palliative team also supports the family, not just the patient

Early palliative referral does NOT mean stopping anti-cancer treatment. It means adding a layer of care focused on symptoms, goals, and quality of life.

### Hospice is appropriate when:

- **Prognosis ≤ 6 months** — estimated by the treating team using clinical judgment (and tools like the Palliative Performance Scale)
- **Further anti-cancer treatment unlikely to help** — or burden outweighs benefit
- **Patient and family are oriented toward comfort** — and have (or are moving toward) an understanding of the trajectory
- **Location-of-care decision has been made or is being made** — home, inpatient hospice, hospital hospice unit

Hospice in China is expanding rapidly but unevenly (see `hospice-in-china.md` for policy and resource detail).

## Common misconceptions

**"缓和医疗 = 临终关怀"**

Wrong. Palliative ≠ hospice. Palliative is a broader category that includes hospice; hospice is the late-disease subset. Referring to palliative care as if it were hospice delays beneficial care by months or years for patients who could be living better right now.

**"Palliative referral means my doctor is giving up on me"**

No. In many cancer centers internationally, stage IV diagnosis triggers an automatic palliative consult alongside oncology — precisely because palliative care provides value independent of disease-directed treatment. In China this is less routine but is the direction standard of care is moving.

**"Hospice = 等死"**

Not how it's framed by hospice teams. Hospice reframes as: "換一種照顧目標" (a different focus of care). The patient is not abandoned; care continues, but the focus shifts from cure/prolongation to comfort, dignity, and meaningful time.

**"If I go to hospice I can't go back to treatment"**

In principle, patients can leave hospice and resume active treatment if circumstances change. The 6-month expectancy is a guideline, not a contract. That said, the transition is emotionally and logistically nontrivial; discuss with the hospice team.

**"缓和医疗 only treats pain"**

Broader than that. Palliative medicine addresses pain, dyspnea, nausea/vomiting, constipation, fatigue, delirium, anorexia, anxiety, depression, spiritual concerns, family communication, advance care planning, and goals-of-care clarification. It is a full specialty.

**"Home hospice means I won't get medicine / interventions"**

Home hospice typically provides medications for symptom control (including opioids for pain and dyspnea), nursing visits, sometimes volunteer companionship, bereavement follow-up for the family. What is generally stopped is disease-directed treatment (chemotherapy, radiation for life-prolongation rather than symptom relief, ICU-level interventions).

## Practical implications for this skill

When a user asks "我妈是不是该进 hospice 了" — do not answer directly. Instead:

1. Clarify what they mean by "hospice" (often they mean palliative, or they mean "giving up").
2. Share the palliative vs hospice distinction.
3. Assess: ECOG, symptom burden, prognosis estimate (from treating team, not us), patient and family orientation, treatment options still available.
4. Surface both palliative referral and hospice as options — never advocate one.
5. Route to `hospice-in-china.md` if the conversation is genuinely about hospice entry, and to `symptom-management-end-of-life.md` / `pain-management-opiophobia.md` if the driver is uncontrolled symptoms.
