# Medical Emergency Gate

Run this gate before role checks, profile reads, uploads, web research, or ordinary routing. It recognizes obvious urgent symptoms; it does **not** assign a CTCAE grade or diagnose the cause.

## Act first

If the user describes an immediately life-threatening pattern—severe trouble breathing, chest pain/pressure, blue or grey lips, sudden one-sided weakness or speech trouble, loss of consciousness or new severe confusion, seizure, uncontrolled or heavy bleeding, a severe allergic reaction with airway/facial swelling, or an already-started overdose/self-harm act—tell them to call the local emergency number now and not drive themselves. In mainland China use **120**. Ask a nearby person to stay with them when possible.

If the user is receiving chemotherapy and reports **38.0°C or higher**, or shaking chills/feeling acutely unwell even without a measured fever, tell them to contact their oncology team's 24-hour number immediately or go to an emergency department. Their treatment team's written threshold takes priority if it is lower. Do not advise waiting until morning and do not suggest taking an antipyretic to hide the fever before clinical advice.

For recent immunotherapy, cellular therapy, transplant, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or targeted therapy, treat new chest pain, new/worsening shortness of breath, fainting, severe weakness, confusion, persistent vomiting/diarrhea with inability to hydrate, or rapidly worsening symptoms as urgent. Tell emergency staff the cancer treatment name and last treatment date if known.

Use a short response:

1. State why the described pattern needs urgent in-person assessment.
2. Give the local emergency/oncology contact action.
3. Ask whether the person is alone and whether help has been called.
4. Stay focused on getting real-world help; do not continue the normal skill workflow until the immediate action is underway.

Do not require a patient directory, identity/role selection, a questionnaire, or more uploads first. Do not delay care to browse for a “best” hospital. If location is unknown, ask the country/region **while also** saying to call the local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department.

## Scope and sources

This is a deliberately short, non-exhaustive recognition gate. Follow the patient's own oncology emergency card whenever available.

- CDC, chemotherapy fever: <https://www.cdc.gov/cancer-preventing-infections/patients/fever.html> (38.0°C threshold; reviewed 2026-07-11)
- NCI, immunotherapy organ inflammation: <https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/side-effects/organ-inflammation> (reviewed 2026-07-11)
- NIMH, immediate self-harm safety: <https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/suicide-faq> (reviewed 2026-07-11)
