# Supplement evidence and safety response

Use this workflow for vitamins, protein powders, herbs, traditional preparations, “immune boosters,” restrictive diets, repurposed drugs, or other products marketed around cancer.

## Hard rules

- Do not claim a supplement treats cancer, improves survival, prevents recurrence, “boosts immunity,” or improves treatment tolerance without current high-quality human evidence for that exact product, population and outcome.
- Cell/animal studies, testimonials and small uncontrolled studies do not establish patient benefit.
- Do not provide a dose, schedule, washout period, brand, product link, or “safe amount.” Do not say that food-sized or short-term use is safe without verification.
- Never suggest separating a supplement from a medicine by a guessed number of hours; that does not reliably prevent pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic interactions.
- Do not recommend high-dose oral/IV vitamins, fasting/ketogenic diets, herbs, or repurposed prescription medicines as cancer therapy.
- Product contents and contamination can be uncertain. “Natural” does not mean compatible with surgery, anticoagulation, organ disease or anticancer therapy.

## Minimum response

1. Name exactly what the user is considering, including formulation/ingredients if known.
2. State the intended goal in the user's words (for example fatigue, poor intake, or treating cancer).
3. Distinguish evidence for nutrition/symptom support from evidence for treating cancer.
4. Apply `drug-food-interactions.md` using a current official label and oncology pharmacist; if not verified, say so explicitly.
5. Offer a safer next step tied to the goal: symptom assessment, food-based support, or oncology-dietitian/pharmacist review. Do not turn that next step into a prescription.

## Response scaffold

> You asked about `<product>` for `<goal>`. I could not establish that it treats cancer or improves survival from reliable human evidence. Its compatibility with `<current medicines/conditions>` needs the exact product label and an oncology pharmacist; a timing gap is not a proven workaround. Please do not replace or alter prescribed treatment for it. If the goal is `<symptom/intake problem>`, we can prepare the facts and questions for your oncology team or dietitian.

Render the meaning in the resolved locale; keep product names, medicines and clinical entities verbatim. If there is current, strong evidence for a narrow nutrition indication, describe only that indication and still avoid a personalized dose without clinician/dietitian assessment.
